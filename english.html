English lessons for adult beginners

Simple, practical grammar lessons, written for adults learning English from the beginning.

I teach English informally, focused on adult beginners who need clear, practical grammar explanations rather than childish material. Below are a few sample lessons from the program. For the full program and more lessons, visit the link at the bottom of this page.

Lesson 1 · Beginner
The verb "to be"

The verb "to be" changes depending on who you're talking about:

I am a student. You are my friend. He is a teacher. She is at home. It is raining. We are ready. They are here.

Short forms are common in speaking: I'm, you're, he's, she's, it's, we're, they're.

Lesson 2 · Beginner
Asking simple questions

To ask a yes/no question with "to be", move it before the subject:

Are you ready? Is she a doctor? Is it cold today?

To ask for information, add a question word first: What, Where, When, Who, Why, How.

Where are you from? What is your name? How are you?

Lesson 3 · Elementary
Present simple for daily habits

Use the present simple for things that happen regularly — work, routines, facts.

I work every day. She works at a bank. Notice the "-s" added for he/she/it.

To make it negative, use don't or doesn't: I don't work on Sundays. He doesn't like coffee.

Lesson 4 · Elementary
Talking about the past

Regular verbs usually add "-ed" for the past: work → worked, study → studied, watch → watched.

Many common verbs are irregular and must be learned individually: go → went, have → had, see → saw, do → did.

I went to the market yesterday. She didn't go to work last week.
